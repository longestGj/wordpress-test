### 4.2 Direct Answer

**Heading**

`What does non-China titanium dioxide mean?`

**Answer**

`Non-China titanium dioxide is a procurement term used to describe titanium dioxide presented as originating outside China. For procurement teams, the label identifies a sourcing category for due diligence; it does not by itself verify origin, establish how a grade will perform, confirm availability or determine how authorities will treat a particular import. Those questions require separate evidence for the supplier, product, transaction, application and destination.`

**Supporting sentence**

`A sound review starts with the exact sourcing question, then checks each evidence category within its stated scope.`

### 4.3 Six Due-Diligence Checks

**Eyebrow**

`DUE DILIGENCE`

**Heading**

`Six checks before qualifying an alternative-origin source`

**Introduction**

`Use the six checks as a sequence. A strong answer in one area does not replace missing evidence in another.`

#### 01 — Confirm the commercial counterparty

`Record the legal supplier identity, its role in the transaction and the contact channel used for the inquiry. A company name, address or brand identity should not be treated as product-origin evidence.`

**Buyer check:** `Can the supplier and its role in this transaction be identified clearly?`

#### 02 — Confirm the product and grade identity

`Match the offered product name and grade to the technical documents under review. Similar descriptions, shared application labels or origin wording do not establish that two grades are interchangeable.`

**Buyer check:** `Do the offer, technical documents and evaluation sample refer to the same product and grade?`

#### 03 — Verify origin evidence and its scope

`Ask what evidence supports the stated origin and exactly what it covers: a product, lot, order, shipment or other defined transaction. Do not extend an origin statement beyond the scope shown by the evidence.`

**Buyer check:** `Is the origin evidence relevant to the material and transaction being evaluated?`

#### 04 — Review current technical documents and methods

`Check the grade name, document revision, units, test methods and stated conditions before comparing values. Results produced by different methods or under different conditions may not be directly comparable.`

**Buyer check:** `Are the documents current for the grade, and are the comparison methods compatible?`

#### 05 — Define application and processing conditions

`Describe the buyer's formulation or process, operating conditions, performance priorities and validation method. Grade suitability should be assessed against those conditions rather than inferred from origin or a generic application label.`

**Buyer check:** `Are the intended use conditions and acceptance criteria defined?`

#### 06 — Check document, lot and destination requirements

`Identify which records are needed for qualification and how their identifiers relate to the material under review. Separately verify destination-market requirements using current official sources, the relevant product and origin scope, and applicable dates.`

**Buyer check:** `Can the evidence set be connected to the material and the intended destination?`

**Body copy**

### 4.9 Buyer Questions

**Eyebrow**

`BUYER QUESTIONS`

**Heading**

`Buyer questions about non-China TiO2 sourcing`

#### What does non-China titanium dioxide mean in a procurement review?

`It is a procurement term for titanium dioxide presented as originating outside China. It is a sourcing category and due-diligence starting point, not proof of verified origin, performance, availability, equivalence or destination-market treatment.`

#### What should buyers verify before qualifying an alternative-origin source?

`Verify the supplier and its transaction role, product and grade identity, origin evidence and scope, current technical documents, compatible test methods, application conditions, document or lot controls and destination requirements.`

#### Does a different origin make two titanium dioxide grades equivalent?

`No. Origin does not establish equivalent performance or interchangeability. Equivalence requires defined criteria, compatible evidence and validation under the buyer's intended use conditions.`

#### How should company identity, product origin and shipment evidence be handled?

`Treat them as separate evidence questions. A company name or location does not by itself establish the origin of every product, lot or shipment, and one transaction record should not be extended beyond its stated scope.`

#### What technical information should buyers compare?

`Compare current grade-specific information using aligned units, test methods and conditions. Select the criteria relevant to the buyer's formulation or process and define acceptance criteria before qualification.`

#### How do application and processing requirements affect the review?

`They determine which properties, documents and validation steps matter. Begin with the intended system and process rather than selecting a grade from an origin label or generic application category.`

#### Which documents should buyers request?

`Request the records needed to establish product identity, technical and safety context, applicable quality or lot information, origin scope and destination requirements. Confirm availability and applicability for the specific material and transaction.`

#### How should EU, UK, India or Brazil trade context be checked?

`Use current official sources for the intended destination and verify the exact product, origin, procedure and dates. Do not infer a current outcome from a general origin description or an undated summary.`

#### What should buyers do after completing the review?

`Continue to product review when the evidence scope and application criteria are clear. Request the missing evidence when a gap is identifiable, or hold the decision when the evidence cannot support qualification.`
