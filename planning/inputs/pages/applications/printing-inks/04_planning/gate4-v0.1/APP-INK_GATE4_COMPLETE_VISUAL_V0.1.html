PRINTING INKS APPLICATION

Titanium Dioxide for Printing Inks

Compare a TiO2 candidate in the white ink and print you actually need. Define the vehicle, printing process, substrate, deposited film, drying or cure, end use, incumbent and rejection conditions before interpreting opacity, dispersion or cost.

Build Your Print Comparison · Review Printing Inks Grades

Define the ink and print system

Start with the system in which the pigment will be evaluated. Record what you know about:

  • ink chemistry and vehicle;
  • printing process and relevant equipment context;
  • substrate and any surface treatment;
  • deposited white-ink film and how it is prepared;
  • drying or cure conditions;
  • end use and any later converting step that affects the evaluation;
  • incumbent ink or pigment reference;
  • optical and colour targets; and
  • rejection conditions for the printed result.

If a detail is unknown, state that it is unknown and identify the person or supplier who may hold it. You can still prepare a focused request without guessing a process, Grade or target.

Judge opacity in the printed white layer

Opacity is a result of the prepared ink, deposited layer, substrate and measurement basis. Keep it separate from coverage or pinholes and from colour. A more opaque reading does not by itself show that the white layer is uniform or that the overprinted colour result is acceptable.

A 2023 Applied Sciences study examined a solvent-based opaque white reverse-flexographic layer on treated BOPET while varying anilox volume under otherwise stated print conditions. It assessed opacity and ink-layer uniformity, including pinholes, separately. The study did not compare TiO2 candidates, and its measurements were taken before varnishing and lamination. Its results therefore illustrate why deposited-layer conditions belong in the comparison; they do not supply a target for another ink, press, substrate or converted structure.

ISO 23498:2022 describes a method for visual opacity assessment of printed white ink on transparent and opaque substrates. ISO 13655:2017 covers spectral measurement and colorimetric computation for graphic-arts images. Use the full method selected by your laboratory and record the specimen, backing, instrument conditions and acceptance basis. These public scope pages do not provide a universal opacity threshold or convert a printed-ink colour result into a raw-pigment whiteness result.

Read dispersion, rheology and storage together

Dispersion state, viscosity and stability can move together or trade off under a particular formulation and method. Record the vehicle, pigment state, dispersant context, solids basis, preparation, shear or measurement conditions and observation period before comparing results.

A 2025 ACS Omega study investigated aqueous dispersions of untreated primary rutile TiO2 with poly(acrylic acid) sodium salt dispersants of different molecular weights. It measured viscosity, zeta potential and stability with multiple-light-scattering methods in that research system. This is evidence that dispersion, rheology and stability should be read together. It is not a finished commercial printing ink, a surface-treated Grade comparison, a dispersant recommendation, a dosage instruction or a shelf-life claim.

When results differ, first check whether the preparation, solids basis, temperature, shear history, method or observation period also changed. Do not assign the difference to the pigment alone unless the comparison supports that conclusion.

Keep grind and particle measurements in context

ISO 1524:2020 covers the use of a graduated gauge to determine fineness of grind for paints, inks and related products. A fineness-of-grind gauge reading is taken under specified conditions at the groove depth where discrete solid particles become readily discernible. It is not the pigment's primary particle size, a complete particle-size distribution or proof of nozzle, screen or filtration performance.

Record the full method, sample preparation, gauge range, reading basis and units. Pair the result with the rheology, stability and printed-film evidence needed for your own system rather than treating one grind reading as a complete dispersion verdict.

Use supplier documents to frame the comparison

Record What it can support What to check
Current TDS Grade-level screening information Product identity, revision, method, units and whether a value is typical or specified
SDS Safety communication Correct product identity and revision; it is not an application-performance report or a compliance conclusion
Relevant lot CoA Results for an identified lot Lot identity, method, units and scope; it does not establish finished-ink or print performance by itself
Application or trial data Evidence for a tested system Vehicle, process, substrate, deposited film, drying or cure, reference, method and endpoint

Select the records that answer the question you are working on. A TDS may support early Grade screening, while an SDS provides safety information. Add lot evidence when a lot is identified and application data when a tested system needs to be assessed. Record missing context for later verification; incomplete records do not require you to invent a conclusion.

Compare incumbent and candidate on matched conditions

Keep the incumbent and candidate trials aligned wherever the comparison depends on the same condition. Record formulation basis, preparation, print method, substrate, deposited film, drying or cure, conditioning, measurement method, reference and acceptance requirement. If one factor must change, disclose it and judge what the resulting comparison can still answer.

ISO 2834-2:2022 covers preparation of test prints with liquid printing inks for processes including flexography and gravure. Its public scope includes water-based, solvent-based and radiation-curing inks and excludes inkjet inks. It does not declare a pigment suitable for a process or provide a complete production setting.

Assess opacity, coverage or pinholes, colour, rheology, stability and other required print or conversion results as separate stated endpoints. If a result reaches its acceptance requirement, decide whether a representative repeat or next test is needed. If information is missing, record the question and repeat or extend the relevant test. If a result does not reach the requirement, retain that result for the declared system. These outcomes do not recommend or rank a Grade for another system.

Compare cost on accepted printed output

Price per kilogram, nominal TiO2 content and ink loading form a meaningful cost comparison only after both the incumbent and candidate inks reach the same accepted endpoint in the declared printed output.

Use your own current delivered prices, actual consumption, accepted print yield, waste, rework, downtime and allocated trial or change costs. Keep currency, date, commercial terms and destination comparable. If the technical endpoint or current cost inputs are not aligned, collect the missing evidence instead of projecting a saving or a Grade winner.
