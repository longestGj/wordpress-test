Product positioning

Where M-350 Fits in Product Evaluation

M-350 is a general-grade rutile titanium dioxide pigment for buyers evaluating one Grade across several formulation types. The M-350 TDS describes particle-size control, inorganic treatment with aluminum and zirconium compounds, and compound organic treatment.

Use M-350's listed features and technical data to decide whether it should enter testing for your coating, ink or plastics system. Final Grade selection should account for the complete formulation, pigment loading, dispersion process, substrate or polymer, application method, processing conditions, service environment and destination-document requirements.

Application directions

Application Directions to Evaluate

Decorative Coatings

Evaluate M-350 in the intended decorative coating formulation. Confirm hue, gloss, hiding power, dispersion and finished appearance at the planned pigment loading, film build and application conditions.

Industrial Coatings

Review M-350 in the complete industrial coating system. Test dispersion, hiding target, surface appearance and finished performance using the intended substrate, curing or drying process, and service conditions.

Automotive Coatings

Include M-350 in formulation testing for the intended automotive coating system. Confirm color direction, gloss, hiding power, dispersion and finished-film performance under the actual application and curing conditions.

Printing Inks

Evaluate M-350 in the buyer's ink formulation and printing process. Review dispersion, color and hiding targets, print appearance and compatibility with the selected vehicle, additives and production conditions.

Plastics

Test M-350 in the intended polymer formulation and processing system. Confirm dispersion, opacity and appearance at the planned pigment loading, additive package, processing temperature and end-use conditions.

Additional Paper Evaluation Path

Paper is an additional evaluation path for M-350. The M-350 TDS does not provide paper-specific performance detail, so confirm the intended paper furnish, coating or converting system, processing conditions and target properties during technical review.

Formulation evaluation

What to Evaluate in Your Formulation

Optical and Dispersion Review

  • Hue and gloss against the finished-appearance target
  • Hiding power at the intended pigment loading and film, print or polymer conditions
  • Dispersibility in the selected binder, ink vehicle or polymer system
  • Finished appearance and consistency under the intended production process

Durability and Process Qualification

  • Weather-resistance requirements under the intended service conditions
  • Temperature-resistance requirements under the actual processing and end-use conditions
  • Compatibility with the complete formulation, additives, substrate or polymer
  • Application method, process settings, destination requirements and sample-validation results

These priorities identify what to test during buyer evaluation. They do not guarantee a fixed result in every formulation, process, application or service environment.
